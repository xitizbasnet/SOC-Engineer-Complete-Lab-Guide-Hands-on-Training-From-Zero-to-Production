# Module 4: SOC Operations

## Alert Triage, Threat Intelligence & Playbook Execution

The SOC (Security Operations Centre) is the central hub for monitoring, detecting, and responding to cybersecurity threats in real time. Effective SOC operations require disciplined processes, clear ownership, and data-driven decision making.

---

# Lab 4.1 — SOC Workflow & Alert Triage

## Objective

**Process alerts using a structured triage methodology.**

---

## Step 1: Alert Queue Management

1. Open the SIEM/SOAR queue:

   * **Splunk ES**
   * **Microsoft Sentinel**
   * **ServiceNow SecOps**

2. Prioritise alerts in the following order:

   **Critical → High → Medium → Low**

   > ⚠️ **Priority:** Never reverse this order.

3. Check whether the alert is:

   * A scheduled false positive.
   * Generated by a known-good internal tool.

4. Assign the alert to yourself.

   > 🔐 **Ownership:** Never leave alerts in limbo without ownership.

5. Apply the following SLA targets:

   | Priority     | SLA Target |
   | ------------ | ---------: |
   | **Critical** |  `<15 min` |
   | **High**     |    `<1 hr` |
   | **Medium**   |    `<4 hr` |
   | **Low**      |   `<24 hr` |

---

## Step 2: Triage an Alert — Structured Approach

Use the following five-step methodology when investigating an alert.

### Step 1 — Validate

Determine whether the alert is firing correctly.

* Verify the alert logic.
* Check the health and availability of the associated data source.

### Step 2 — Context

Establish the context surrounding the alert:

* Who is the user?
* What is the affected host?
* What is the asset criticality?
* Which business unit owns the asset?

### Step 3 — Enrich

Query available threat intelligence sources, including:

* **VirusTotal**
* **AlienVault OTX**
* **TalosIntel**

### Step 4 — Correlate

Determine whether additional alerts involve the same:

* User
* Host
* IP address

### Step 5 — Decide

Determine the alert disposition:

```text
True Positive → Escalate
False Positive → Document & Tune
```

> 💡 **Triage Principle:** Make the disposition decision based on validated evidence, contextual information, and correlated activity.

---

## Step 3: Escalation Matrix

### L1 — First-Level SOC

L1 handles:

* Known alert patterns
* Standard playbooks
* Routine false positives

### L2 — Second-Level SOC

Escalate to L2 when:

* Unknown malware is identified.
* A compromise is confirmed.
* Lateral movement is detected.

### L3 — Third-Level SOC

Escalate to L3 when:

* APT indicators are identified.
* Data exfiltration is detected.
* A critical asset is compromised.

### CISO / Management

Escalate to CISO/Management when:

* Ransomware is identified.
* A breach notification trigger occurs.
* Regulatory impact is identified.

> 📝 **Documentation Requirement:** Always document the escalation reason and timeline.

---

## ⭐ Best Practice Tips

* **Never close an alert without documenting:** the decision made, evidence reviewed, and next steps.
* **Context is everything** — a failed login from an IT administrator is different from a failed login on a service account at 3 AM.
* **Maintain shift handover notes** — every analyst must know what was worked on before their shift.
* **Measure MTTD (Mean Time to Detect) and MTTR (Mean Time to Respond)** — track weekly trends.
* **Rotate analysts between alert types** to prevent tunnel vision and build broader skills.
